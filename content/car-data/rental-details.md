# Rental Terms & Conditions — Reference Guide (Morocco)

> Content/reference document for the Fider Media & Brand team. Describes conventional
> rental-terms fields relevant to the Moroccan market, for use in marketing copy, demo
> content, contract templates, and onboarding materials. Reference-quality market
> information, not a legal document and not pulled from a specific agency's real contract.

## 1. Minimum driver age

- **Standard minimum age:** 21 years old for Economy/Compact categories at most agencies.
- **Category-restricted minimum age:** 23–25 years old is commonly required for SUV, Van/
  Minibus, and especially Luxury/Premium categories.
- Renters under the standard threshold but above an agency's absolute floor (commonly 21)
  are usually still eligible, subject to the young-driver surcharge described in
  `pricing-structure.md` §6.

## 2. Minimum years licensed

- **Typical minimum:** 1–2 years of continuous, valid driving license history.
- Agencies commonly require the license to be held for at least 1 full year at minimum,
  rising to 2–3 years for higher-risk categories (SUV, Luxury/Premium, Van/Minibus).
- License must be valid for the full duration of the rental.

## 3. Required documents

**Moroccan residents:**
- **CIN** (Carte d'Identité Nationale) — original, valid.
- Valid Moroccan or internationally recognized driving license.

**Foreign visitors:**
- **Passport** — original, valid for the duration of the stay.
- **Valid driving license** from the renter's home country, generally required to have been
  held for at least 1 year.
- **International Driving Permit (IDP)** — commonly requested alongside the home-country
  license, especially for licenses not in French/Arabic/Latin script, though enforcement
  varies by agency.
- Proof of onward travel or hotel/return booking is sometimes requested informally but is
  not a universal requirement.

Both resident and foreign renters typically provide a **local contact phone number and
address (or hotel)** for the duration of the rental.

## 4. Accepted payment methods

Commonly accepted in the Moroccan agency-rental market:

- **Cash (MAD)** — still widely used, especially for smaller/independent agencies and
  security deposits.
- **Bank card** (Visa/Mastercard, local and international) — increasingly standard for both
  payment and deposit hold at established agencies.
- **Bank transfer** — common for corporate/B2B accounts (see Hub context) and pre-paid
  online bookings.
- **Mobile payment** — a growing but not yet universal option in this market.

Security deposits are conventionally taken by **card hold/authorization** where possible
(cleaner reversal at return) or cash where card facilities aren't available.

## 5. Insurance tiers

Three broad tiers are conventional in this market — framed here as **capabilities a rental
contract commonly offers**, not as claims about a specific certified product:

| Tier | Coverage | Typical renter liability |
|---|---|---|
| **Basic (CDW baseline)** | Collision Damage Waiver at a base level — covers major accident damage | A deductible/excess typically equal to a portion of the deposit remains the renter's responsibility |
| **CDW (standard)** | Reduced deductible on collision damage; often the agency's default included tier | Lower out-of-pocket exposure than Basic |
| **Full coverage** | Deductible reduced to zero or near-zero; often bundled with theft protection and sometimes tire/windshield coverage | Minimal to no renter liability for covered incidents |

Full coverage is typically offered as a paid upgrade over the base tier bundled into the
daily rate. Exact terms (what's excluded — e.g. undercarriage damage, off-road use, driving
under the influence) are contract-specific and should always be described accurately per the
actual agency contract, never generalized as "full protection."

## 6. Fuel policy

- **Full-to-full** is the standard convention: the vehicle is handed over with a full tank
  and must be returned full. This is the fairest and most common policy in the Moroccan
  market and the one to default to in marketing copy.
- Where a renter returns the vehicle with less fuel than received, agencies typically charge
  the shortfall at a per-liter rate that includes a service premium above pump price.
- Some agencies offer a "full-to-empty" or pre-purchased tank option; this is less common and
  generally less favorable to the renter, so it shouldn't be presented as the default.

## 7. What the deposit covers

As described in `pricing-structure.md` §3, the deposit (HOLD at pickup → RETURN or CLAIM at
drop-off) conventionally covers:

- New damage to the vehicle identified by comparing the pre-rental and post-rental
  inspections (paint, bodywork, glass, interior, tires/rims).
- Missing or below-agreed fuel level (under a full-to-full policy).
- Uncleared traffic fines or toll charges incurred during the rental period and later
  attributed to the vehicle.
- Cleaning fees for excessive soiling beyond normal use.
- Missing accessories/equipment provided with the vehicle (spare tire, jack, documents,
  child seat if rented, etc.).

It does **not** typically cover normal wear and tear, or damage the renter purchased full
coverage against (see §5).

## 8. Cancellation policy conventions

Typical market bands (illustrative — actual policy is set per agency/booking):

| Notice before pickup | Typical outcome |
|---|---|
| 48+ hours | Full refund of any prepayment |
| 24–48 hours | Partial refund (commonly 50%) or credit toward a future booking |
| Under 24 hours / no-show | Prepayment forfeited or a cancellation fee equal to one day's rate |

Deposits (as opposed to prepaid rental fees) are not charged until pickup, so cancellation
before pickup does not involve a deposit release/claim — only the resolution of any advance
booking payment.
